# UIE Assignment Cheat Sheet

Built from the 130 NPTEL assignment questions (noc26_de26, Weeks 0–12) — the terms they actually name, not the whole syllabus.

## 1. The named models

The ones that appear as a *term* you either know or don't.

| Term | What the assignment wants | Asked in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inverted Pyramid Model** | Flip the hierarchy so the customer is on top → **improved customer focus by prioritizing their needs**. The decoy is "concise messaging with a clear audience focus" (that is the journalism sense). | W12 Q5 |
| **Beachhead market** | Win one narrow, focused segment first, then expand. Opposite of spreading resources thin. | W12 Q3 |
| **Problem recognition scale** | **Active/urgent** = aware *and* actively seeking a solution. **Passive** = aware but no motivation to change. Third rung: not aware at all. | W4 Q9, Q10 |
| **Outside-in thinking** | Build to customer requirements rather than internal engineering. Module 4's slide lists it as a *pitfall*, but NPTEL grades it as the customer-driven answer. | W4 Q2 |
| **Synectics** | Creative method connecting unrelated concepts via analogy. Four analogies: **personal** (imagine yourself inside the problem), direct, symbolic, fantasy. Goal = break conventional thinking. | W7 Q2, W10 Q1, Q8 |
| **Fractional ownership** | Shared ownership of a high-value *physical* asset (NetJets). Not digital goods, not IP. | W7 Q7 |
| **Prior art** | Previously granted patents + published applications. Searched to establish novelty and non-obviousness. | W3 Q10 |
| **Regulatory barriers** | Standards and compliances protecting public health — and a barrier to entry. | W3 Q7 |
| **TRL** | Technology Readiness Levels, 1–9, concept → market. | W5 Q8 |
| **Proof of Concept** | Demonstrates **functional feasibility** — not scalability, not patents, not beauty. | W5 Q9, W10 Q6 |
| **MVP** | Just enough features for early adopters to use and give feedback (Eric Ries). Validates the idea. | W8 Q4 |
| **Commercialization** | Transferring an idea to industry via IP or technology transfer. Distinct from *incubation* (business plan + resources) and *entrepreneurship* (own it yourself). | W9 Q9, Q5 |
| **Clinical immersion** | Observing in a hospital to find unmet needs — not training, not marketing. | W9 Q7 |
| **Inductive reasoning** | Specific observations → general conclusion. Deductive = reverse; abductive = best explanation. | W10 Q4 |
| **Prototyping methods** | Additive, subtractive, forming, plus CNC/3D printing. **Lateral thinking is not one** — that's the trick option. | W10 Q2 |
| **Design brief / need statement** | **Functional requirement + user requirement**, plus pain points, user journey and ecosystem fit. Never budget or timeline. | W3 Q5, W6 Q6, Q9 |
| **Design management** | A *structured process* for fulfilling business objectives through design. | W6 Q7 |
| **Lean Canvas** | Ash Maurya's modification of Osterwalder's BMC. Lets you test and iterate assumptions fast; one per customer segment. | W7 Q10, W8 Q5 |
| **Early adopter** | Dire need **and** willingness to experiment. Gives critical feedback, validates demand. | W2 Q5, W8 Q1 |
| **UVP** | Attracts *and retains* customers by clearly addressing their needs. | W8 Q10 |
| **IPR types** | Patents, copyrights, trademarks, trade secrets. Secure **before commercializing**. Licensable by startups, industry *and* NGOs — "all of the above". | W5 Q6, Q7, W8 Q7, Q8 |

## 2. Week 0 basics

- IPO = Initial Public Offering
- SWOT: **T = Threat**
- KPI = Key Performance Indicator
- **Trademark is not a business structure**
- OYO + Swiggy = both **unicorns** ($1 bn+ valuation), not "both tech startups"

## 4. Answer heuristics

Measured across the 130 questions:

- **"All of the above" was correct 10 times out of the 11 it appeared.** The exception is W10 Q9 (entrepreneur collaboration → "sharing resources and distributing production responsibilities", because "competing in the same market segments" sat inside the catch-all and is wrong).
- **The correct option is the longest one in 63 of 125 single-answer questions** — double chance. Weak alone, decisive as a tie-breaker.
- The right answer is almost always **customer-centric, evidence-based, iterative or collaborative**. Options saying *ignore feedback, rely on instinct, skip research, focus solely on profit / technology / aesthetics* are never correct — not once in 130.
- **5 questions are multi-select**: W1 Q9 and Q10, W2 Q9, W4 Q5, W9 Q3. In W1 Q9 all four options were correct; in the others the wrong options contain "solely", "ignoring" or "limited".
