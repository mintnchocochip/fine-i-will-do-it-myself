# UIE Exam Notes — Understanding Incubation & Entrepreneurship

Consolidated from the 24 course lecture decks (Modules 1–12) and the 130 NPTEL assignment questions. Module N = Week N.

★ = high-yield, appeared in or directly underlies an assignment question.

---

## 0. The answer method

Before the content, the filter that resolves ~90% of questions:

1. **Kill any option containing** *solely, only, ignore, avoid research, rely on instinct, skip, without validation, limited, guarantees, vague, exaggerated, rigid, random, at the expense of*. Across 130 real questions these were correct **zero** times.
2. Among survivors, prefer the option naming the **customer, user, or evidence** over one naming an internal benefit (cost, speed, aesthetics, technology, convenience).
3. If two or more survivors are all genuinely positive and **"All of the above"** exists → pick it (right 10 of 11 times).
4. Still tied → pick the **longest** option (right 63 of 125 times).

## 1. Module 1 — Evidence-Based Entrepreneurship

★ **Lean Startup = three components**

| Component | Person |
| --- | --- |
| Business Model Generation | Alexander Osterwalder |
| Customer Development | Steve Blank |
| Agile Engineering | Eric Ries |

★ **Essence of the Lean Startup**
- Technology is **not** why startups fail
- A startup is **not a small version of a large company**
- Customers do not want technology — they want **needs satisfied**
- A startup is a **temporary organisation searching for a scalable and repeatable business model**
- You must **step out and seek evidence**
- It does **not guarantee success — it reduces the odds of failure**

★ **Why startups fail** (from 101 startup post-mortems): **no market need** (#1) · ran out of cash · not the right team · got outcompeted. 50% don't survive 6 months, 90% don't survive a year. *"Startups fail because they fail to get customers."*

★ **Common founder mistakes:** overestimating the number of customers · misjudging urgency of the need · building without talking to customers · talking to the **wrong** people · forgetting non-product aspects (workflow, channels, revenue model, partners).

★ **Three questions for any innovation:** Who needs it and why? · Can you deliver that value? · Can you make enough money?

★ **The Startup Story, 5 stages:** Dream → Disruptive Idea → Team Building → Business Model → Launch.

★ **Evidence-based entrepreneurship** = validated learning through experimentation and data · the **Build–Measure–Learn** cycle · decisions from empirical evidence, not assumptions · continuous customer engagement. (An assignment made *all four* of these correct simultaneously.)

**Startup vs large company**

| Scalable startup | Large company |
| --- | --- |
| Searches for unknown customers | Executes known processes |
| Learns unknown needs | Has a playbook |
| Works from guesses | Needs mostly followers |
| Unknown environment | Known environment |

*"A business model is more useful for a startup than a business plan."*

**Case — MedGyor (Dinoj Joseph):** v1 sterile surgical enclosure, killed by evidence (surgeries in medical camps banned, rural volumes too low) → pivoted to an affordable burns isolation room. 140+ customers interviewed across 10 states. Lesson: *always ask whether they will buy, and at what price*.

## 2. Module 2 — Hand-holding for Entrepreneurship

★ **Customer discovery rules**
- Customers do **not** care about features, technology, trends or buzzwords — only the solution to their problem
- People are busy: they won't use your product **even for free** if there's no need
- **Talk to customers and strangers. Do not talk to friends and family about your product**
- **Don't talk about your product — listen**
- Ask **open-ended** questions; don't lead; don't listen with a filter
- A problem is **what they keep ranting about**, not what you ask them
- Know the difference between a customer **complaining** and a customer **needing**

★ **Pivot** when the product isn't fulfilling its purpose.

★ *"Money is not the cause why startups die. It is the product–market fit."* Funding never justifies success. Learn to say no **and** to hear no.

★ Deal with customer inertia by finding the right **niche** and identifying **early adopters**.

★ Steve Blank: *"Customer discovery translates the founder's mission into reality."*

**Cases:** Musthafa P.C. (iD Fresh Food) — key innovation was **software to organise the supply chain**, not the batter. Anurag → Neodocs (at-home diagnostics). Amit Srivastava → InfyU Labs (food quality). Mayuri Chopra → Roha Biotech (**biodegradable packaging replacing styrofoam**).

## 3. Module 3 — Innovation Method, Teams, Problem Statements

★ **The 7 C's of Innovation** (in order)

| # | Concern | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Cause** | The resolve to solve a problem | Vein Tracer |
| 2 | **Context** | Understanding the problem space and environment | Jiva water-filter bottle |
| 3 | **Comprehension** | Arriving at design insights | Solar oven — people want to cook **from indoors**; sold as a "rice cooker", not an "oven" |
| 4 | **Check** | The list of key checks = the product brief | Palki — cut weight 30–40% |
| 5 | **Conception** | Generating ideas, combining into concepts | Helmet clusters |
| 6 | **Crafting** | Mock-ups → functional prototypes → mass production | India Post letterbox (old one looked like a **dustbin**) |
| 7 | **Connection** | Delighting the user | Z-Line petrol pump |

★ **Empathy** is where design starts — it produces **user-centred design**. The Vein Tracer began by visiting blood banks with no product in mind.

★ **Team building**
- *"It's not what stream you are from, it's what hat you wear in the team"*
- **Lower your egos, be flexible, decide your role**
- **Ideal team = at least 4 people**; more diverse is better
- Choose either **aligned goals** or **diverse roles**

★ **Prior art** = previously granted patents and published patent applications. Searched because patentability needs **novelty and non-obviousness**.

★ **Regulatory barriers** = standards, legal requirements and compliances protecting public health — and strong enough to **deter new entrants**.

★ **Need statement / design brief** = **functional requirement + user requirement**. Be specific; add a persona.

★ **Think product category, not single product** — gives you Plan B, C, D (risk diversification and adaptability).

★ **Problem statement:** define the problem clearly before solving. Frame with **Who / What / Where / Why / When**. Example redefinition: *"help a visually impaired person be self-reliant"* → *"reduce the dependency of the visually impaired while making transactions for purchased goods."*

*"Without collaboration, innovation cannot move forward."* Innovation inside an organisation is driven by **interdisciplinary collaboration**.

## 4. Module 4 — Customer Discovery

★ **Start with the team, not the idea.** Investors walk away when they don't see a strong team. *"The goal is not to make the idea successful, but to make the team successful."*

★ **Pitfalls named:** inventor syndrome · "we just need investors" · outside-in thinking.
*(Note: the assignment treats **outside-in thinking** as creating products that cater to customer requirements, and marks that correct. Go with the customer-centric reading in an MCQ.)*

★ **Rules**
- You **cannot convince everyone at the beginning** — focus on a **single persona**
- Find customers with the **sharpest need**
- **Visionary customers** have a need so sharp they try to solve it themselves before a solution exists — the best place to start
- **Focus on the problem, not the technology**. Never pitch on the basis of solution technology
- Understand the **entire customer journey** to know where design should intervene
- Separate **customer (who pays)** from **user (who uses)** — toddler feeding: customer = parents, user = toddlers
- People may not realise they have a problem until it's too late
- With multiple personas, never solve one group's problem using another group's data

★ **Problem recognition scale**

| Stage | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Urgent / active** | Recognises the problem **and is actively seeking a solution** |
| **Passive** | **Aware of the problem but has no motivation to change** |
| **Unaware** | No awareness that a problem exists |

★ **Value proposition is not always fast, cheap or good design** — different users have different motivations (a pen for a student ≠ a pen for a CEO).

**Case — Solar oven:** segments were low-income rural and middle-income small-town, later health-conscious professionals. Channels: direct sale, **Krishi Vigyan Kendras**, **government renewable-energy schemes**, institutes/NGOs. Relationships: cooking demos, food festivals, recipe shows, Mahila Mandals. Revenue: direct sale, bulk government orders, **subsidies for eco-friendly products**. Claim: saves **40–50% of LPG** cost.

★ **Promoting sustainable products:** awareness via influential figures/partnerships, interactive demonstrations, community education. Affordability via direct sales without intermediaries, government subsidies, bulk institutional orders. Rural distribution via **government schemes**.

## 5. Module 5 — Bio-Medical Innovation

★ **R.E.INV.ENT** — Academia (Researcher → Educator) produces **knowledge**; Society (Innovator → Entrepreneur) produces **products**.

★ **Valleys of Death** (TRL 1–9: Concept → PoC → Prototype → Product → Market)

| Valley | Crossed by | Funding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ideation | R&D laboratory | ₹50,000 |
| Invention | Prototyping centre | ₹5 lakh |
| Innovation | Technology acceleration | ₹50 lakh |
| Impaction | Business incubation (plan, mass production, distribution) | ₹5 crore |

★ **TRL = Technology Readiness Levels.** Left of the line = academia, right = society.

★ **Medical device risk classes:** I bandages/thermometers · II needles/syringes · III ventilators/bone plates · IV implants/pacemakers. Purpose of a device: **diagnosis and treatment**.

★ **The 4 D's**

| Phase | Owner | Contains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Define** | Doctor | Team building, **clinical immersion**, problem definition, concept & feasibility |
| **Develop** | Researcher | Detailed design, virtual/rapid/functional prototypes |
| **Deliver** | Entrepreneur | Pilot manufacturing, pre-clinical testing, clinical trials, certification |
| **Deploy** | Investor | IP, business model, mass production, distribution |

★ **Within Define:** domain familiarisation (**physiology = normal organ; pathophysiology = disease state**) · **clinical immersion** with permission and consent, observing diagnosis/treatment/follow-up · **functional requirements — "need to have, not nice to have"** · concept generation via **divergent thinking** to break the *creative thinking barrier*, then **convergent thinking**.

★ **Feasibility test — four circles:** significant local need · value proposition of **5–10× benefit** · capability and feasibility · committed team. *(Government subsidy is the classic wrong option here.)*

★ **Deliver:** lab testing establishes **"reasonable evidence of safety" before human trials**. Human clinical trials prove **safety, efficacy and accuracy**. Certification depends on **risk class**.

★ **Deploy:** product dossier = design + manufacture (**ISO 13485**) + validation. IP filed by the institute, revenue shared with researchers. Licensing to startups, industry **and NGOs** — all are eligible.

★ **Secure IPR before commercialising** the product.

★ **Pilot production** exists to **test and adapt design features from user feedback**.

★ **Proof of Concept** demonstrates **functional feasibility**.

★ **Telemedicine** during the pandemic **reduced strain on physical healthcare facilities**.

★ Investors are reluctant on healthcare hardware because of the **poor success rate of medical devices**.

★ Best practice for functional requirements: **thorough stakeholder interviews**.

**Ikigai / Champions:** Passion (love + good at) · Mission (love + world needs) · Vocation (world needs + paid) · Profession (good at + paid).

## 6. Module 6 — New-Age Entrepreneurship & Design Management

★ **What enterprise clients look for: Trust, Quality, Speed, Transparency.**

★ **Human-centred engagement** = building empathy and conducting user research (not lean tools, not traditional marketing).

★ **Aligning product with expectations** = iterative testing and feedback loops.

★ **Design thinking, user research and strategy** together **link business operations with innovation efforts**.

★ The design researcher's objective: **balance user needs, technological feasibility and business objectives**. Three lenses = **business viability + human desirability + technical feasibility**.

★ **Interview technique:** build **rapport and trust** first. Start from primary/secondary/tertiary objectives → research questions → methods. **Never ask a 'DO' question or a 'WHY' question.** Listen and probe at the right time.

★ **Robust design brief** = user pain points + user journey map + **ecosystem fit**. A comprehensive brief includes **specific user personas and their pain points** — not timelines, budgets or marketing plans.

★ **Design management** = **a structured process for fulfilling business objectives through design.**

★ **Empathising with both users and business goals** balances **creative ideas with realistic objectives**.

★ **Frame the problem as a problem** — not as a way to validate the idea you already have.

★ Getting past jugaad/copy-paste: change the business aspiration → disruptive idea → **reframe the problem** → identify the capability gap.

**Context:** enterprise software = large footprint (HRMS, CRM), serious users, legacy products, reluctance to shift. The **squiggly line of innovation** shows innovation is iterative, not linear. Retainers give a dedicated team, deep dives and cost transparency. WestJet case: ticket closure time cut **80%**.

## 7. Module 7 — Business Models

★ **The four business models**

| Model | Definition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Win–Win** | All parties benefit, no losers; product becomes a platform/ecosystem | Deaf & mute wristband → APIs + cloud + home automation; Practo |
| **Double Whammy** | **Paid from both ends of the value chain** | Jawed Habib Hair Xpress; Duolingo |
| **Fractional Ownership** | **Shared ownership of a high-value physical asset** | NetJets |
| **Perceived Shortage** | Manufacture scarcity to spike demand | Xiaomi flash sales; Zara's 90-day cycle |

Zomato is a **trader's model**, not a double whammy.

★ **Sweet spot** = **user needs ∩ operational/technological possibilities ∩ business goals**. *"Pivot till you reach the sweet spot."*

★ **Three critical factors in product development: market need, technical feasibility, business viability.**

★ **Business Model Canvas** = the tool that **graphically represents a business model** so stakeholders grasp it easily.

★ **Lean Canvas** — created by **Ash Maurya**, a modification of the BMC. Value: lets startups **test and iterate business-model assumptions quickly**. One Lean Canvas **per customer segment**; combine them into **one BMC**.
- Lean Canvas = a **thinking tool**; BMC = a **communication tool** for investors and mentors
- Sequence: **6 Questions → Lean Canvas per segment → one BMC**

★ **The 6-Question (5H–1W) framework:** How do we reach the customer? · optimise operations? · source manpower? · source inputs? · **What** tie-ups should we seek? · How do we create an optimal product mix?

★ **Channels — three senses:** delivery, marketing, **payment**.
★ **Key metrics — three types:** revenue, customer acquisition, operational.
★ **Early adopter** = **dire need + willingness to experiment.**
★ **Criteria of a good business model:** clear value proposition, scalability, sustainable revenue streams.
★ **Monitoring metrics:** revenue growth, customer acquisition, customer retention.

★ **Synectics** = generating ideas by taking **analogies from unrelated areas** (direct and indirect). *"To be creative, put the watchman in your mind to sleep."*

★ **Lean Startup cycle:** after building an MVP you **measure customer feedback and learn from the results**.

★ Collaborative models give **access to resources + networking + community** (all of the above).

~45% of startups fail because they cannot achieve product–market fit. Z-Line pump: **75% cheaper**, best-selling pump of its decade. Innovation model = user insights + new technology + creative ideation, with feedback at every stage.

## 8. Module 8 — Incubation, Lean Canvas, Atomberg

★ **Role of an incubator:** select and direct new businesses into profitable arenas, hand-hold early, raise funds at the right stages, make the connections essential to survival. Offers infrastructure, funding, mentoring, training, advisory (legal/IP/finance), visibility and networking.

★ **SINE** — a **Technology Business Incubator**, **not-for-profit**, **self-sustained through revenue and equity sharing by incubatees**, **3-year** incubation. Selects **IP-driven, futuristic startups beyond proof-of-concept**; **avoids aggregator platforms and low entry-barrier technologies**.

★ **Spin-out preparation — four topics**

**(a) Intellectual Property** — by default IP in academia is owned by the **host institute**; seek a licence/technology transfer; guides can waive rights for royalty or a stake; co-inventors not joining must be compensated for their **NOC**; **patent before disclosing**.

**(b) Team** — a startup is **never a single person's journey**; build complementary skills and shared vision. The **founders' agreement** defines roles, commitment of money and work, **equity sharing by value/commitment/risk**, **proportional dilution** when a co-founder joins, and that a departing co-founder cannot take full equity.

**(c) Customer discovery** — the product **will not sell by itself**; do market sizing and competition analysis.

**(d) Fundability — four sources**

| Source | What they want |
| --- | --- |
| **Government** | The **first risk capital in the country**; supports high-risk innovation **without expecting return** |
| **Angels / VCs** | Return on capital, a **scalable** business, partial exit in **3–4 years** |
| **Corporate strategic** | **Strategic/synergistic relevance** — market sizing is *not* the priority |
| **Family business** | Acquire as an asset class; exit in **7–8 years or more** |

★ **Three traits of an entrepreneur:** entrepreneurial mindset (risk over a long journey) · **flexibility** (no emotional attachment, able to pivot) · perseverance (values and ethics, no shortcuts).

★ **MVP** = *"a version of a product with just enough features to be usable by early customers who can then provide feedback for future product development"* (Eric Ries). Its role: **attract early adopters and validate the product idea**.

★ **Build → Measure → Learn.** Lean Canvas helps **verify the idea**, **focus on the customer**, and "**sell before you build**".

★ **Innovation adoption curve:** Innovators → Early Adopters → Early Majority → Late Majority → Laggards.

★ **Unfair advantage / competitive landscape** = what **cannot easily be copied or bought** — IP, cost structure, network effects, data, exclusive contracts.

★ **Early adopters** let you **validate market demand through early feedback**.

★ **A compelling pitch needs a scalable and profitable business model.** Break the journey into **milestones** and raise to reach the next one. Elevator pitch **under 50 words**; **18-month** milestone plan.

★ **Intellectual property** = legal protections preventing unauthorised use of original creative works. Types: **patents, copyrights, trademarks, trade secrets**.

★ **Customer acquisition** = acquiring new customers **efficiently and cost-effectively**.

★ **A strong UVP** attracts **and retains** customers by clearly addressing their needs.

★ **Staying competitive** requires **consistent product improvement and innovation**.

★ A **red ocean strategy** (competing in existing industries) is not ideal for tech startups. Decide to **persevere or pivot**.

**Atomberg:** founders Manoj Meena and Sibabrata Das; three levers = **innovation, design, digital-first distribution**; BLDC motors, **65% more energy efficient**, Atomsense algorithm kept as a **trade secret**.

## 9. Module 9 — Entrepreneurship in Academia

★ **Entrepreneur vs innovator:** entrepreneurs **see problems as business opportunities**; innovators focus solely on solving the problem. Entrepreneurs **look ahead and build creative ways** to solve a need. They need **different aptitudes and skill sets**.

★ **Two paths from an idea**

| Commercialisation | Entrepreneurship |
| --- | --- |
| Give the idea to industry | Own it yourself |
| via **IP / technology transfer** | via the incubation cell |

★ **Commercialisation** = transferring an idea to industry through **intellectual property or technology transfer**.

★ **Entrepreneurial journey, 5 stages:** **Germination** (inclination, basics, own idea) → **Pre-Incubation** (ideation to prototype) → **Incubation** (prototype to viable product; **register the startup here**) → **Accelerator** (scale and sustain) → **Host** (commercially successful company).

★ **Incubation stage purpose:** developing a business plan and securing resources.

★ **Idea Generation Stage** = the initial creation and development of the startup idea.

★ **The Value Net** — four players around your venture: **Customers** · **Suppliers** · **Substitutes/competitors** (SEGA to Nintendo) · **Complements/complementors** (EA, whose games make the console valuable).

★ **Dell case:** USP = the **customisation facility**; challenges were component inventory, turnaround time, cost of customisation, and direct model vs retail model.

★ **Barriers to turning an idea into action:** lack of confidence · fear of negative feedback · difficulty securing funding (**all of the above**).

★ **The most important relationship for a startup to manage: target customers.**

★ **Early-stage funding typically sought:** government grants · seed funding and crowdfunding. (*Not* long-term high-interest loans.)

★ **Why regions lag in techno-entrepreneurship:** education focused only on jobs · abundant resources reducing the push to innovate · discouraging government policy (**all of the above**).

★ **Clinical immersion** exists to **identify unmet needs and gather insights for innovation**.

★ **Better execution** comes from a **clear definition of customer needs and market gaps**.

★ A startup must have **commercial potential** — "AI-based image processing algorithm" was rejected; "water-triggered point-of-use heating device for trekkers" was accepted.

## 10. Module 10 — PoC, Prototyping, Frugality

★ **Synectics — the four analogy types.** Goal of Synectics: **encourage exploration and break conventional thinking** by connecting unrelated concepts.

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Direct** | Compare the problem to how nature or another industry already solves it (the spoon inspired by a mobile phone) |
| **Personal** | **Imagine yourself as part of the problem or object** — "if I were the spoon in a child's hand" |
| **Symbolic** | Compress the essence into a short, often paradoxical phrase, like a book title — "controlled collapse" |
| **Fantasy** | Wish-fulfilment ignoring physics or cost — "the spoon feeds the child itself" — then pull back to reality |

Chakravarthy groups these as **direct and indirect** analogies; personal, symbolic and fantasy are the indirect ones. **"CAD analogy" is not a real type** — it is the invented distractor in the Week 10 question.

★ **Reasoning:** **inductive** = specific observations → general conclusion. Deductive = general → specific. Abductive = best available explanation. A physical prototype works on **all three**.

★ **Proof of Concept** = making sure the imagined concept **will work**, and communicating it. **The first PoC is a physical sketch of the concept in your mind.** It is raw, frugal and cheap, **not expected to look finished** — say so when showing it. Purpose: **validate feasibility and viability**.

★ **A prototype is a communication tool.** Forms: visual/virtual (2D sketches, 3D CAD) · physical (volume, size, tactile, weight) · as a process (iteration, evaluation, feedback, optimisation) · functional (technology, ergonomics, pilot).

★ **Why prototype:** save time, money and effort · prove radical concepts and new technology · evaluate design · **identify and resolve design flaws early**.

★ **Three types of prototype:** **idea** (crude, quick & dirty, PoC, rigs) · **scale** (look-like, mock-ups) · **working** (functional, pilots).

★ **Prototyping methods:** **additive** (clay, POP, rubber) · **subtractive** (styrene, styrofoam, PU foam) · **forming** · plus CNC, laser cutting, 3D printing. **Lateral thinking is NOT a prototyping method** — a known trap option.

★ **Product development sequence:** **Ideation → Concept Generation → Prototyping → Testing → Market Launch.**

★ **Why clusters?** Every product combines many ideas and features; **clusters help find those features**. Flow: idea generation → cluster formation → cluster representative → idea development → concepts → exploratory models → mock-ups → selection → refinement → prototype.

★ **Frugality in innovation** benefits a firm by **minimising waste and reducing cost through efficient use of resources**.

★ **ART of Frugality — three frugalities:** for the **customer**, for the **manufacturer**, for **Mother Earth**. Frugality alone is not enough — it needs **sustainability and inclusivity**. The sachet is inclusive but not frugal once collection cost is counted; dynamite fishing is frugal but not sustainable.

★ **Accessibility – Affordability – Availability** must all hold (Mitticool is accessible and affordable but has only 18–20 outlets).

★ **How entrepreneurs collaborate:** **sharing resources and distributing production responsibilities** — mutual franchise, pooled catalogue, pooled inventory and procurement, single bid with multiple suppliers, **distributed manufacturing**. Note: *competing in the same market segment is not collaboration*, which is why "all of the above" was **wrong** on this one.

★ **Autopoiesis** = the liberty of a design to **improve with usage**: regeneration, repair/replace, rejuvenate, bypasses.

★ **Honey Bee Network** gives grassroots innovators **Visibility, Voice, Velocity**. Frugal innovators share **passion, purpose, focus, process, performing through platforms**. KPI in this world = the **value created in society**.

★ **Pester Power** = children's ability to pressure parents into buying advertised products.

**NPD** = new product development: bringing a new product to market, renewing an existing one, or taking a product into a new market. *"Technology is not a barrier, it is a tool."* *"Design never ends — every evolution is a pit stop."*

## 11–12. Modules 11 & 12 — Pitching and Inclusive Design

★ **Pitch structure:** 1 Team → 2 Problem → 3 Customer → 4 Solution/Product → 5 Canvases (Lean Canvas per segment + combined BMC) → 6 Feedback → 7 The Startup Pitch.

★ **Value Proposition Canvas**

| Product side | Customer side |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Customer jobs |
| Gains | Gain creators |
| Pains | Pain relievers |

★ **KPI funnel: SEE → THINK → DO → CARE.** Output KPIs: awareness → brand searches → financial KPIs → **repeat purchase rate**.

★ **Reviewer takeaways** (these map directly onto Week 12 answers)
- *"Focus on what is the real value proposition."*
- *"Any VC will want to know how they will make money — have one or two concrete examples."*
- *"Try to get specific, narrow, choose one or two segments."* → **beachhead market**
- Be clear on **who pays**, not just who uses
- Validate a proposed product range with **consumer research**
- A single subscription platform can serve segments with different needs

★ **Beachhead market** — win one focused segment first, then expand. Not spreading resources thin.

★ **Inverted Pyramid Model** — flipping the usual hierarchy so the **customer sits on top**: the advantage is **improved customer focus by prioritising their needs**. *(NPTEL-confirmed. The tempting wrong option is "prioritizing concise messaging with a clear audience focus" — that is the journalism sense of the term, not the one this course means.)*

★ **Modular and customisable products** → **increased customer satisfaction and efficiency** from one platform.

★ **Networking** works when used for **targeted connections** that reach customers, mentors and investors.

★ **Early problem identification** gives a **well-defined direction for development**.

★ **Lean marketing** for startups = **innovative and lean strategies**, not expensive or aggressive campaigns.

★ **Assistive and inclusive design**
- The right approach for specially-abled users is **user-centred design**
- Two aims of assistive technology: **making users independent** and **fostering social integration**
- Human-to-human interaction beats AI here because it **fosters empathy and understanding of individual needs**
- For children, design that **encourages physical movement and coordination** builds cognitive, motor and analytical skills
- Evidence-based learning: **understand target customers and measure the impact of the solution**

★ **Product success factors:** being **relevant and differentiated**. Offer investors and customers a **unique and differentiated** product.

★ **Avoid cheap materials in B2C** — they **increase return and servicing costs**.

★ **Business-model risk:** **reliance on a small number of corporate clients**.

★ **Aligning with customer needs** secures **long-term satisfaction and loyalty**.
